She stood at the summer field's edge, ice cream melting in her hand,
watching fireflies rise and vanish into dark she didn't understand.
She learned to chase what flickers — to follow light that will not stay,
that travels not as wave alone, nor particle, but both ways.

The mountains do not ask permission when they lift against the sky.
The river finds the sea by falling, not by asking why.
Something old and patient in the world calls out without a sound —
she followed it through every door, through every lab and town.

As night gathers, vast and wordless around her,
she is still the little girl at the field's edge, following the light.
